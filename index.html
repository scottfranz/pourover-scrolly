A pour-over log

122 bags through a V60

Since July 2024 I've logged every bag of filter coffee I've brewed: roaster, origin, process, how funky it tasted, and a rating out of ten.

Every cup starts at my grinder, a 1Zpresso ZP6, with 15 g of beans. Here each bean stands for one bag in the log.

Scroll to brew.

Grind

Turn the crank

Pour-over wants a medium-fine grind: coarser than espresso, finer than a French press. On my ZP6 that's usually between 4.0 and 5.5 on the dial.

Into the V60

Grounds in, kettle ready

The grounds settle into a paper filter, and the gooseneck kettle comes in.

0:00–0:45 · Bloom

Wet the grounds first

A small pour, about three times the coffee's weight in water, lets trapped CO₂ escape. Fresh coffee puffs up and bubbles.

My median bag rested 14 days after roasting before I opened it.

0:45–2:00 · Main pour

Slow spirals, out and back

Every bag got a rating once it was finished. The median is 7.7, and half of all ratings land between 7.3 and 8.1.

By process

How the cherry was processed

Washed coffees rate higher on average than naturals: 7.71 (n=77) against 7.18 (n=17). Experimental processes average 6.98 (n=21).

Honey (7.58, n=5) is left off so the chart stays readable with three shapes.

The funk cliff

Funky is fine, until it isn't

Ratings hold steady through moderate funk, then fall off at clean/funk 8+: 7.66 for bags at 6 or below (n=108), 4.58 for 8 and up (n=6).

The high-funk group is small, so treat its shape as a sketch.
